                   WILBUNS-5: Entity Alias Resolution Standard
                 Project Morningside / Constance Architecture
                              Category: Standards Track
                              Published: 22 November 2025


Status of This Memo

   This document specifies the alias-resolution procedures for the
   Wilbuns Taxonomy Normalization System (“Wilbuns”). It defines the
   requirements for handling non-canonical entity mappings, contextual
   alias determination, submission of new canonical entities, and
   evidence obligations. Distribution of this memo is unlimited.

Abstract

   WILBUNS-5 establishes the authoritative process by which applications
   MAY perform local alias resolution and tentative entity mapping, while
   ensuring that canonical entity assignments remain stable, curated, and
   fully governed. This document clarifies that non-canonical mappings,
   including potential aliases, ambiguous matches, and proposed new
   entities, SHALL be implemented and tracked at the application level,
   and SHALL NOT modify the Wilbuns canonical entity registry unless
   submitted with justification and deterministic evidentiary support.
   Such evidence includes, but is not limited to, notability guidelines
   comparable to Wikipedia standards for natural persons, EIN or state-
   issued identifiers for corporate entities, and analogous authoritative
   references. As with prior Wilbuns standards, implementers are reminded
   that attempting to reverse poor entity merges often resembles running
   a locomotive on fractured track, un-neutering a K9, or reconstituting
   spilt milk: arduous, inadvisable, and rarely successful.

1.  Introduction

   Wilbuns assigns canonical identifiers (e.g., ENT:000492114) to
   entities including people, organizations, facilities, parcels,
   places, and related objects. Because real-world data contains
   abundant variation in names, spellings, abbreviations, and
   ambiguous forms, applications MUST perform alias evaluation,
   contextual disambiguation, and probabilistic matching when
   processing documents.

   This standard defines:

       (1) The difference between canonical and non-canonical entity
           representations.

       (2) Requirements for local (application-level) alias tracking.

       (3) Rules for proposing new canonical entities or confirmed
           aliases into the Wilbuns registry.

       (4) The evidentiary standard for acceptance.

   Canonical entries are managed centrally; aliases and tentative
   matches are managed locally unless formally submitted and approved.

2.  Canonical Entities

2.1 Definition

   A canonical entity is a single, authoritative entry in the Wilbuns
   ENT domain representing a real-world person, organization, place, or
   other identity-bearing object. Canonical entities MUST have:

       - A stable Wilbuns ID (ENT:<number>)
       - A definitive human-readable name
       - Supporting metadata and descriptive text
       - Evidence sufficient to justify permanent inclusion

2.2 Immutability

   Canonical entity records SHALL be treated as permanent archival
   entries. Changes to name, description, or associated metadata SHALL
   follow the governance procedures defined in WILBUNS-4 and SHALL NOT
   be performed automatically by downstream systems.

3.  Non-Canonical Entities and Aliases

3.1 Definition

   A non-canonical entity refers to any form or string that resembles an
   entity name but does not yet meet the requirements for canonical
   inclusion. Examples include:

       - Partial names (e.g., “Abbitt”)
       - Variant spellings (e.g., “W. M. Abbitt Jr.”)
       - Abbreviations (e.g., “W. Abbitt”)
       - Machine-generated candidates from OCR
       - Mistyped or noisy strings

3.2 Contextual Aliases

   Applications MAY treat a non-canonical form as an alias of a known
   canonical entity if contextual signals (e.g., geography, document
   type, temporal proximity) provide sufficiently high confidence.

   Applications MUST track local resolution decisions and associated
   signals but MUST NOT write or promote contextual aliases into the
   canonical registry without a formal submission.

4.  Application-Level Alias Tracking

   Implementations MUST:

       - Maintain a local alias table to store possible matches.
       - Store probability scores, evidence, and contextual cues.
       - Avoid writing these entries into Wilbuns canonical space.
       - Ensure reversibility when new information appears.

   A local alias mapping MAY be used for processing and inference but
   SHALL NOT be treated as authoritative across systems.

5.  Submitting New Canonical Entities or Permanent Aliases

5.1 Submission Requirement

   When an application determines that a non-canonical alias SHOULD
   become a canonical Wilbuns entity or SHOULD be permanently bound as an
   official alias of an existing Wilbuns entity, the implementation MUST
   submit a request to the Wilbuns governance process.

   Each submission MUST include:

       - Proposed canonical label
       - Evidence supporting identity or equivalence
       - Contextual match logs
       - Deterministic matching factors
       - Source citations

5.2 Evidence Standards

   The following evidence SHALL be considered sufficient:

       - **Natural persons:** Documentation comparable to Wikipedia
         notability guidelines (public office, publications, verifiable
         biography, public records, etc.)

       - **Corporate entities:** EIN, state corporation commission ID,
         statutory registration, or publicly verifiable corporate record.

       - **Government units:** State code references, municipal
         charters, or authoritative mapping to recognized jurisdictions.

       - **Facilities/locations:** GIS authority datasets, postal
         records, or government facility registries.

   Weak, unverifiable, or ambiguous evidence SHALL NOT be accepted.

5.3 Deterministic Match Evidence

   Submissions MUST provide deterministic evidence, such as:

       - Textual match thresholds
       - Named entity confidence scores
       - Geographic consistency
       - Document topology consistency
       - Historical continuity (e.g., an official known to serve a
         region appearing in records from that region)

   Submissions lacking such evidence MAY be rejected or deferred.

6.  Alias Resolution Workflow

   Applications SHOULD follow this workflow:

       1. Extract candidate names or strings.
       2. Perform probabilistic alias matching.
       3. Apply contextual filters (geography, domain, time).
       4. Select the highest-confidence canonical match.
       5. Store the result locally with supporting signals.
       6. Optionally submit confirmed aliases to Wilbuns authority.

   Applications MUST retain the possibility of reversal until the
   alias or new entity is formally accepted into the canonical registry.

7.  Security Considerations

   Misidentification of persons or organizations can result in
   incorrect linkage of records, denial of rights, reputational harm,
   or archival corruption. Alias resolution MUST be conservative,
   reversible, and thoroughly documented. Implementers SHOULD exercise
   care equivalent to handling legal identity documents.

8.  IANA Considerations

   This document requests no actions from IANA. The Wilbuns Entity
   Registry SHALL be maintained internally.
