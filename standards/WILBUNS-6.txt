                 WILBUNS-6: Authorized Entity-Application Registry
                 Project Morningside / Constance Architecture
                              Category: Standards Track
                              Published: 22 November 2025


Status of This Memo

   This document defines the Wilbuns Authorized Entity-Application
   Registry (AEAR), hereafter referred to as “the Registrar.” The
   Registrar provides a formally governed namespace for identifying,
   authorizing, and managing the entities and software applications
   permitted to issue Wilbuns-compliant envelopes, submit canonical
   taxonomy proposals, and participate in standards-governed
   environments, including government, statutory depository, and life-
   safety contexts. Distribution of this memo is unlimited.

Abstract

   WILBUNS-6 establishes the rules, formats, and governance mechanisms
   for the Authorized Entity-Application Registry. The Registrar assigns
   unique Entity-Application Identifiers (AEIDs), records metadata
   associated with participating organizations and applications, and
   defines the permissions associated with each AEID. Only registered
   AEIDs are authorized to (1) generate Wilbuns-compliant envelopes, (2)
   submit canonical ID, alias, or taxonomy proposals, or (3) operate in
   regulated environments. Applications MAY maintain private alias
   resolution or local taxonomy extensions under their assigned AEID, but
   SHALL NOT modify the canonical Wilbuns taxonomies without formal
   submission. As with prior Wilbuns standards, this document accepts
   that inappropriate authorization or misallocation of AEIDs may prove
   difficult or impossible to reverse—akin to running railcars upon
   broken track, attempting to un-neuter a K9, or reconstituting spilt
   milk after the bottle has shattered.

1.  Introduction

   The Wilbuns taxonomy ecosystem requires a mechanism to identify and
   authenticate the organizations and applications permitted to
   participate in canonical ID issuance and standards-governed
   operations. The purpose of the Authorized Entity-Application Registry
   is threefold:

       (1) Identify and authorize trusted entities and applications.
       (2) Provide a permanent, auditable registry of participants.
       (3) Enable per-application namespaces for local alias and taxonomy
           management without polluting the canonical spaces.

   The AEAR ensures that only vetted, reviewable participants may
   contribute to canonical taxonomies or generate envelopes used in
   regulatory, statutory, archival, or life-safety environments.

2.  Definitions

2.1 Entity

   A legal or natural person (e.g., Morningside Group LLC, a government
   agency, or an accredited archival institution). Entities MUST be
   unique and SHALL be assigned an Entity ID (ENT entry), separate from
   any AEID.

2.2 Application

   A software system operated by an Entity. Applications MUST have a
   distinct name and SHALL be assigned an Application ID.

2.3 AEID (Entity-Application Identifier)

   A unique identifier representing a specific authorized application
   operated by a specific entity. Format:

       AEID:<number>

   Each AEID is globally unique and SHALL NOT be reassigned.


3.  The Entity Index (`entities_index.json`)

3.1 Purpose

   The `entities_index.json` file SHALL provide an authoritative,
   machine-readable registry of all entities recognized by the Wilbuns
   standards body. This registry is distinct from `registrar.json`, which
   governs entity-application pairs. The entity index provides the
   metadata foundation necessary for admissions, auditing, provenance,
   and canonical decision-making.

3.2 File Location

       /registrar/entities_index.json

   A JSON Schema SHOULD be provided at:

       /registrar/entities.schema.json

3.3 Required Fields

   Each entry in `entities_index.json` MUST contain:

       - entity_id (canonical ENT ID)
       - entity_name
       - entity_type (“company”, “government”, “individual”, etc.)
       - admission_date (ISO 8601)
       - jurisdiction
       - status (“active”, “revoked”, “suspended”)

   Recommended additional fields:

       - entity_url
       - poc_name (point of contact)
       - poc_email
       - authorized_scopes (entity-level)
       - external_ids (EIN, SCC-ID, LEI, etc.)
       - reason_for_admission
       - public_key (optional for future signature systems)

3.4 Normative Usage

   Entries in `entities_index.json` represent entities authorized to
   operate Wilbuns-compliant applications or submit canonical updates.
   Applications MUST NOT treat entries in this index as canonical entity
   definitions; only ENT-domain entries qualify as canonical entities.

   The entity index SHALL serve as a cross-reference for AEID allocation,
   eligibility review, and canonical submission auditing.

3.5 Immutability

   Entity index entries SHALL NOT be removed. Revoked or inactive
   entities MUST remain listed with updated status fields.
   
4.  Registrar Structure

   The Registrar SHALL be stored at:

       /registrar/registrar.json

   along with a JSON Schema located at:

       /registrar/registrar.schema.json

   Implementations MUST use this file to determine authorization status.

4.1 Registrar JSON Format

   Each entry SHALL contain:

       - entity_name
       - entity_id (canonical ENT ID)
       - application_name
       - application_id (APP prefix)
       - entity_application_id (AEID)
       - admission_date (ISO8601)
       - authorization_scope (array)
       - contact (optional)
       - public_key (optional for signing)

   The file SHALL contain an array of such records.

5.  Authorization Scope

   Each AEID SHALL have one or more of the following scopes:

       - envelope-write
       - canonical-write
       - alias-submit
       - taxonomy-proposal
       - statutory-depository
       - commercial-use
       - research-only
       - life-safety

   The Registrar MAY define additional scopes through future standards.

6.  AEID Allocation

6.1 Assignment

   AEIDs SHALL be assigned sequentially from a dedicated range:

       AEID:900000 – AEID:999999

   The Registrar MUST ensure no collision or duplication occurs.

6.2 Immutability

   Once assigned, an AEID SHALL remain permanently associated with the
   entity-application pair. Revocation SHALL mark the AEID inactive but
   SHALL NOT reuse its numeric value.

7.  Local Namespaces for AEIDs

7.1 Local Aliases

   Authorized applications MAY create local alias resolution tables
   stored at:

       /applications/AEID-<number>/aliases/

   These aliases MUST NOT be considered canonical. They MAY be used
   internally or for envelope processing.

7.2 Local Taxonomy Extensions

   Applications MAY maintain local taxonomy supplements at:

       /applications/AEID-<number>/local_taxonomies/

   These MAY include internal labels, experimental categories, or pre-
   normalized values. Such terms SHALL NOT enter the canonical Wilbuns
   taxonomies without formal submission and approval.

7.3 Local Metadata

   Metadata associated with AEID operations SHOULD be stored at:

       /applications/AEID-<number>/metadata/

8.  Submitting Canonical Proposals

8.1 Eligibility

   Only AEIDs with “canonical-write” or “taxonomy-proposal” scope MAY
   submit:

       - new canonical entity proposals
       - canonical alias proposals
       - new taxonomy values
       - structural changes (subject to governance review)

8.2 Evidence Requirement

   Submissions MUST include the deterministic match evidence and
   justification described in WILBUNS-5, including:

       - notability criteria for natural persons
       - EIN, state ID, or equivalent for organizations
       - statutory authority for government units
       - GIS or facility registry entries for locations

8.3 Review and Decision

   The Registrar SHALL publish review guidelines under /governance/.

   Accepted entries SHALL be added to canonical registries through
   versioned updates. Rejected entries MAY be resubmitted with additional
   evidence.

9.  Revocation and Sanctions

   The Registrar MAY suspend or revoke AEIDs that:

       - submit fraudulent or misleading proposals
       - repeatedly violate Wilbuns standards
       - generate non-compliant envelopes in regulated contexts

   Revoked AEIDs SHALL remain in registrar.json with “active: false”.

   

10.  Security Considerations

   AEID misuse can compromise the integrity of canonical taxonomies,
   produce misleading historical records, or affect life-safety
   applications. Implementers MUST ensure strong operational security and
   SHOULD use cryptographic signatures when submitting canonical changes
   (to be specified in a future standard).

11.  IANA Considerations

   This document requests no IANA actions. The Wilbuns Registrar SHALL
   be maintained internally.
